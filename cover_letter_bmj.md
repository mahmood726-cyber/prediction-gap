Please consider the manuscript "The Prediction Gap: 70% of Statistically Significant Cochrane Meta-Analyses Have Prediction Intervals That Include the Null" as a Research Article in The BMJ.

Clinical guidelines and evidence summaries usually treat a statistically significant pooled effect as reassuring evidence that the intervention will work in practice. This study tests that assumption by comparing confidence intervals with prediction intervals across 403 Cochrane meta-analyses from the Pairwise70 dataset. Among reviews with a 95% confidence interval excluding the null, 69.8% still had a prediction interval that included the null, indicating that apparent average benefit often fails to translate into confidence about the next clinical setting.

The manuscript is relevant to BMJ readers because it addresses how clinicians, guideline panels, and reviewers interpret meta-analytic evidence in real-world decision making. The main implication is practical rather than theoretical: prediction intervals should be reported routinely when random-effects meta-analysis is used.

The manuscript has not been published or submitted elsewhere. The author has approved the submitted version. The analysis code and output data are publicly available.
